The problem

Dogs and cats can damage the same door seal in different ways.

Weather stripping sits exposed along the door jamb, right where paws and claws can reach it. Repeated scratching can scuff, tear, pull, or wear down that soft seal material.

Dogs: repeated pawing at the doorway

Some dogs scratch or paw at a door when they want to go outside, come inside, or get your attention. That repeated contact can land directly on the exposed weather stripping and surrounding jamb.

Cats: claws meet a tempting vertical edge

Scratching is a normal feline behavior, and some cats prefer vertical surfaces. An exposed door edge can become a repeated target, putting the soft weather stripping directly in the path of sharp claws.

Different behavior. Same vulnerable weak spot.

Once the seal is torn or pulled loose, the doorway can look damaged and the weather stripping may need repair or replacement. Scratch Guard puts a clear protective surface between your pet and that vulnerable edge.

Why the seal area matters

Protecting weather stripping is about more than appearance.

Weather stripping helps limit air movement around a closed door. When pets repeatedly scratch that edge, the damage can affect both the look of the doorway and the condition of the seal.

FAQ

Common questions.

What does Scratch Guard protect?

Scratch Guard covers the vulnerable door-jamb and weather-stripping edge where dog paws and cat claws can repeatedly contact and damage the seal area.

What size is Scratch Guard?

Each Scratch Guard unit is 32 inches long and made of clear acrylic. It's designed to cover the typical scratch zone on a standard door frame.

Is Scratch Guard for both dogs and cats?

Yes. Dogs may paw or scratch at a door when they want in or out, while scratching is a normal behavior for cats and many cats prefer vertical surfaces. Scratch Guard is designed to protect the vulnerable weather-stripping edge from either type of repeated contact.

Is Scratch Guard visible after installation?

Scratch Guard uses a clear acrylic profile designed to remain visually subtle once installed.

How is it attached?

Scratch Guard uses strong adhesive backing for a straightforward peel-and-stick installation. For best adhesion, repair any loose or chipping paint, rot, damaged wood, or deteriorated trim first, then apply Scratch Guard to a clean, dry, solid surface.

Can Scratch Guard be removed?

Scratch Guard uses a strong adhesive designed to stay in place long-term. If you need to remove it later, the adhesive may require careful work—using a plastic scraper, heat gun, or adhesive remover. Some residue or minor surface touch-up may be needed depending on your door frame's finish. We recommend testing in an inconspicuous area if you're concerned about your specific surface.

Is this suitable for renters?

Scratch Guard can help renters protect door frames from pet damage during their tenancy. However, because the adhesive is designed to stay put, removal may require effort and could potentially affect some surfaces. If you're renting, check with your landlord or test in a small area first.
